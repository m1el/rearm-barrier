### 🤖 **Assistant**  ·  19:35:58; model 13.1s

**A receiver can check ownership for one use of a buffer, then read data from its next use.** The ownership check and sequence check are separate, and the buffer can be recycled between them.

For example, with one buffer and two receiver threads:

1. Sender publishes chunk **0**.
2. Receiver B, waiting for chunk **1**, reads `client_owned == true`, then pauses.
3. Receiver A consumes chunk **0** and returns the buffer.
4. Sender reuses it, writing chunk **1**, its length, and sequence number.
5. B resumes. It reads sequence **1**, finds its ticket matches, and reads the payload.

B’s acquire synchronized with publication **0**, not the writes for chunk **1**. Its relaxed sequence load supplies no new synchronization. **The payload read therefore lacks the required happens-before relationship with its writer—a data-race problem.** B can even reach the callback before the sender publishes ownership for chunk 1.

There’s a second problem with simply strengthening the sequence store/load: B could finish and return ownership **before** the sender’s delayed `owned = true`. That store would overwrite B’s acknowledgement and potentially strand the buffer.

The underlying defect is that **generation selection and ownership handoff aren’t tied together**. Cannoli’s multiple receiver threads make this schedule relevant.

The Lean witness verifies the generation mismatch; the executable clock model confirms the missing synchronization. A production repair hasn’t been implemented yet.
